A dental practice should reply to a new enquiry within minutes, and ideally within the first minute. Not by the end of the day, not after lunch, and not "first thing tomorrow". When someone fills in a form or messages you, they're thinking about their teeth right now, and they've probably contacted two or three other practices at the same time. The first one to answer properly usually gets the booking. Everyone after that is chasing someone who has already moved on.

I say this to practice owners all the time, and most of them nod and then tell me their reply time is "pretty good". Then we send a test enquiry at 7pm on a Tuesday and wait. Sometimes the reply comes the next morning. Sometimes it's two days later. Sometimes it doesn't come at all, because the form went to an inbox nobody checks anymore.

That's not a lazy team. That's a busy team with no system behind them.

## Why the first few minutes matter so much

Think about what's happening on the other side. Someone has a chipped tooth, or they've finally decided to look into implants, or they need a check-up before their health fund resets. They search, they open a few practice websites, and they fill in a couple of forms.

At that moment they're ready. They have their phone in their hand and they're expecting an answer. Give it an hour and they're back at work, picking up the kids, or cooking dinner. The urgency fades, and so does their memory of which practice was which.

So when your reply turns up the next morning, you're not the practice they were excited about. You're one of several messages in their inbox, and if another practice has already booked them in, yours gets ignored.

Speed isn't about being pushy. It's about being there while the patient still cares.

## What "fast" actually looks like

Here's the bar I'd set for any practice:

1. **New web form or message: within a few minutes.** Even if it's just a real reply that confirms you've got it, asks one or two useful questions, and offers a time.
2. **Missed call: a text back straight away.** Plenty of people won't leave a voicemail. If the phone rings out and nothing happens, they just call the next practice on the list.
3. **After hours: still a reply.** A lot of enquiries come in at night and on weekends, because that's when people have time to think about it. They shouldn't hear nothing until Monday.
4. **Not ready yet: follow up, don't forget.** Some people are comparing options or waiting on payday. They need a few friendly check-ins over the next couple of weeks, not one message and silence.

Notice that none of this needs a long reply. A quick, human message that answers their question and makes booking easy beats a polished essay that arrives tomorrow.

## Why practices struggle with this

It's almost never a people problem. Your front desk is answering the phone, checking patients in, sorting out payments and dealing with the health funds. A web enquiry sitting in an inbox is easy to miss when there's a person standing at the counter.

The other common gaps:

- **Enquiries land in too many places.** The website form goes to one email, Facebook messages go to someone's phone, Google messages go somewhere else again. Nobody sees all of them.
- **Nobody owns follow-up.** Everyone assumes someone else replied.
- **No after-hours cover.** The practice closes at 5:30 and so does the reply.
- **Hot and cold enquiries get the same treatment.** Someone in pain today and someone casually asking about whitening prices end up in the same pile.

If you're spending money on [Google Ads for your practice](../dental-google-ads.html), this is where a lot of it quietly leaks out. The ads did their job and the enquiry came in. It just wasn't answered in time.

## How to reply fast without burning out your team

You don't need someone glued to the inbox. You need a few things set up properly.

**Put every enquiry in one place.** Forms, social messages, missed calls, all landing in one view your team checks.

**Send an instant first reply.** Not a cold "we've received your message" auto-responder. Something that sounds like your practice, asks the questions your front desk would ask, and offers a next step.

**Sort the urgent from the curious.** Someone with a toothache needs a call now. Someone researching veneers can get a friendly message and a follow-up. Your team's time should go to the ones ready to book.

**Text back missed calls automatically.** This one change catches a surprising number of people who would otherwise have rung the next practice.

**Keep following up the ones not ready yet.** A short sequence over a couple of weeks, then hand them to a person when they reply.
