If your practice only shows up when someone types its name, it's because Google knows who you are but isn't sure what you do or where you do it. Ranking for your own name is easy, because nobody else is called that. Ranking for "dentist Crows Nest" or "dental implants near me" means Google has to pick you over every other practice nearby, and it only does that when your listing, your website and your reviews all clearly say what you offer and which suburbs you serve. Most practices stuck in this spot have a thin website, one services page, and a Google listing that was filled in once and left alone.

I hear some version of this every week. "We're number one on Google." Then we search the practice name, and yes, there it is at the top. Then we search the treatment they actually want more of, plus their suburb, and they're nowhere.

Seeing your name at the top feels like proof the website is working. It just isn't the search that brings new patients in.

## Your name isn't a competitive search

Think about who types your practice name into Google. Existing patients looking for your phone number. Someone a friend referred. Someone who saw your sign on the way home. They already chose you. Google is just helping them find the door.

New patients don't search like that. They search for the problem or the treatment, and where they are. "Emergency dentist open Saturday." "Invisalign Parramatta." "Dentist near me." Every practice within a few kilometres is in the running for those, and Google has to decide which three get the map and which ones sit below.

So if your name is the only thing you rank for, the honest read is this: you're visible to people who already know you, and invisible to everyone else.

## Why Google won't put you up for treatments yet

Google is trying to answer three questions about every practice: what do you do, where are you, and do people trust you. Ranking for your name only needs the second one. Ranking for treatments needs all three, and that's where most practices fall short.

### The website has one services page

This is the most common one. A single page that lists implants, crowns, whitening, Invisalign, check-ups and emergency care, with a line or two about each.

To Google, that page is about everything, which means it's not really about anything. When someone searches "dental implants" plus your suburb, Google is looking for a page that's clearly and fully about implants. If a practice down the road has one and you don't, they get the spot, even if you place more implants than they do.

### The listing doesn't say much

The Google Business Profile has a primary category, a services list, a description, photos and hours. Plenty of practices set the category, add a phone number and stop there. Every empty field is something Google has to guess, and Google doesn't guess in your favour.

The services list matters more than people expect. List treatments the way patients say them: "teeth whitening", not "aesthetic enhancement".

### Your details don't match everywhere

If your website, your listing, the health fund directories and an old Facebook page all show slightly different addresses or phone numbers, Google is less sure you're one real practice in one real place. It sounds minor. It's one of the most common reasons a good practice sits lower than it should.

### Reviews have stalled

Google weighs reviews on the map, and patients read them before they call. A practice that got a burst of reviews two years ago and nothing since looks quieter than one that gets a few every month. Ask as part of the normal routine, and reply to every one like a person would.

One AHPRA point while we're here: reviews on your Google listing are fine, but you can't lift them into your ads as testimonials about clinical care.

## What actually changes it

None of this is clever. It's a list of jobs that need doing properly and then keeping up.

1. **Search like a patient.** On your phone, from a few streets away, search each treatment you want more of plus your suburb. Write down who shows up and what their listings and websites have that yours doesn't.
2. **Give each treatment its own page.** Implants, Invisalign, emergency, check-ups, whatever you want to grow. Each one should answer what patients ask: what's involved, roughly how long it takes, and how to book. Mention the suburbs you really draw from, in plain sentences.
3. **Fill in the listing completely.** Right primary category, every treatment as a service, real photos of your team and rooms, accurate hours including holidays.
4. **Fix your details everywhere.** Same name, address and phone number on every site that lists you.
5. **Keep reviews coming in steadily.** A few a month, every month, beats a big push once a year.

## How long it takes

Map pack movement usually shows in six to ten weeks. Bigger treatment terms like "dental implants" take longer, often four to nine months. That gap is why a lot of our clients run [Google Ads](../dental-google-ads.html) at the start. Ads can bring enquiries in within days while the treatment pages build up.

## Why we only take one practice per area

Here's the part that's specific to how we work. When you're trying to rank for "dentist" plus your suburb, you're competing with the practices closest to you. That's a fixed number of spots on the map.

So we won't take on two dental clinics within 7km of each other. If we're building your treatment pages and working on your listing, we're not doing the same for the practice up the road that's going after the same three spots. It's a simple rule, and it's the only fair way to do [dental SEO](../dental-seo.html) for a local business.
