To rank a dental practice on Google Maps, you need Google to be sure of three things: what you do, where you are, and that real patients trust you. In practice that means a Google Business Profile that's fully filled in and in the right category, practice details that match everywhere online, a website with a proper page for each treatment, and a steady flow of reviews you actually reply to. Do those in that order, keep them up, and most practices start moving in six to ten weeks.

That's the short answer. Here's how we work through it with the clinics we look after, step by step.

## Step 1. Check what Google already thinks you are

Before changing anything, search your practice name and then search "dentist" plus your suburb on your phone. Not on the work computer, where Google knows you've been there a hundred times. On your phone, ideally from a spot a few streets away.

Write down where you show up, who shows up above you, and what their listings have that yours doesn't. More photos? More reviews? A booking button? This takes ten minutes and tells you more than most audits.

## Step 2. Get the category right

Your primary category is the single biggest signal you control on the listing itself. If you're a general practice, it should say so. If you're a specialist practice, use the category that matches your registration and nothing broader.

Then add secondary categories only for things you genuinely offer. Piling on categories you don't really do confuses Google and can annoy patients who turn up expecting something you don't provide.

## Step 3. Fill in every field, properly

Hours, holiday hours, services, the booking link, accessibility details, parking, a description written for a person. Every empty field is a question Google has to guess the answer to.

The services list is where most practices fall short. Don't just write "general dentistry". List the treatments people actually search for in your area, worded the way patients say them.

## Step 4. Make your details match everywhere

Your practice name, address and phone number need to be identical on your website, your listing, the health fund directories, Facebook, and any old listing someone set up years ago and forgot about.

This sounds dull because it is. It's also the most common reason a good practice sits lower than it should. If one directory has "Suite 2" and another has "2/", or an old phone number is still floating around, Google is less sure you are who you say you are.

## Step 5. Give the website something to back it up

Google checks the listing against the website. If your listing says you do implants, Invisalign and emergency care, the website needs a real page for each of those, not one services page with a line about each.

Those pages should mention your suburb and the suburbs you actually draw patients from, in plain sentences written for people. A template with the suburb name swapped in doesn't help anyone. Underneath, the site needs the right structured data so search engines can read exactly what the practice does and where it is. This is a big part of what we do on the [dental SEO](../dental-seo.html) side, because the map and the website really are one job.

## Step 6. Build a steady flow of reviews

Reviews help in two ways. Google uses them, and patients read them before they call.

What matters is a steady flow, not a burst. Ask patients as part of your normal routine, at the desk or in a text after the appointment, so it never turns into a once a year push. Then reply to every review, good or bad, like a human being would.

One AHPRA point to keep in mind. Reviews on your Google listing are fine, but you can't lift them and reuse them in your advertising as testimonials about clinical care. Keep them where they are.

## Step 7. Keep the listing alive

A listing with recent photos of your actual team and your actual clinic looks open and cared for. Add new photos every so often, post updates, answer questions people ask on the listing, and keep holiday hours current.

None of this moves the needle on its own in a single week. Together, over a few months, it's what separates the practice in the top three from the one sitting fifth.

## What you can't change

Distance matters, and you can't move your building. A patient searching from the next suburb over will often see whoever is closest to them. That's why the website and suburb pages matter so much: they're how you earn a spot outside your own few streets.

Map pack movement usually shows in six to ten weeks. Bigger treatment terms like "dental implants" take longer, often four to nine months.
