AI assistants recommend the dental practice they're most sure about, so the fixes are all about removing doubt. Make your name, address, phone and treatments match everywhere you're listed. Give each treatment its own page. Put the answer to the patient's question in the first lines of that page. Add the code that spells out what you do and where. And keep a steady flow of real reviews coming in. None of it is expensive or clever. It's just done, or it isn't, and most practices have at least two of the five missing.

Here's how each one works, in roughly the order we tackle them.

## Why "most sure" is the whole game

When someone asks ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI "who's a good dentist near me for implants", the assistant doesn't go and visit every practice. It pulls together what it can find about the practices nearby and names the ones it can describe with confidence.

That means the practice that gets named isn't always the best one. It's the one that's easiest to be sure about. A great clinic with a messy online footprint can lose to an average one with tidy details. The good news is that tidy details are fixable.

## 1. Make your details match everywhere

Start here, because everything else sits on top of it.

Your practice name, address, phone number, opening hours and the treatments you offer should read exactly the same on your website, your Google listing and every directory you appear on. Not roughly the same. The same.

It's common to find an old phone number on a directory, a suite number missing on Google, or a practice name written three different ways. Each one is a small reason for an assistant to be less sure, and less sure means less likely to name you.

The nice part is this is the same job that helps you on the Google map, so it pays twice. If you want the map side of it, we've written up [how to rank a dental practice on Google Maps](how-to-rank-a-dental-practice-on-google-maps.html).

## 2. Give each treatment its own page

An assistant can't confidently recommend you for implants if your website only mentions implants in a list of fourteen services.

Every treatment you want more of should have a page of its own. Implants, Invisalign, emergency appointments, check-ups and cleans, children's dentistry, whatever matters to your books. Each page should say plainly what the treatment is, who it's for, what happens at the first visit and how to book.

Write in actual treatments and actual suburbs. "Dental implants in Crows Nest" tells a machine something. "Smile solutions for the whole family" tells it nothing.

## 3. Put the answer in the first lines

This is where AI search is a bit different from the SEO you're used to.

Patients don't type "dentist Caringbah" into ChatGPT. They ask real questions. Do I need a crown or a filling? Can anyone see me today with a broken tooth? How long does Invisalign take? The pages that get quoted back are the ones that answer those questions straight away, in plain words, before any background.

So look at your treatment pages and ask: if someone only read the first two sentences, would they have their answer? If the page opens with "At our practice we pride ourselves on", the answer is no.

One thing to keep in mind here. Everything on those pages still has to follow AHPRA's advertising guidelines. Answering clearly is good. Promising outcomes or setting up expectations a patient can't count on isn't, and an assistant will happily repeat whatever you've written.

## 4. Add the code underneath

Structured data, also called schema, is code on your website that spells out for machines what you are, where you are, when you're open and what you offer. Patients never see it. Google and the AI tools read it every time they visit.

Older sites often have none, or has a generic "local business" tag with half the fields blank. Getting it right is a technical job, but it's a small one, and it removes a lot of guesswork for the machines.

## 5. Keep real reviews coming, and answer them

A steady flow of genuine Google reviews tells both Google and the AI tools that your practice is active and that real patients trust it. A burst of twenty reviews two years ago and silence since says the opposite.

Make it easy for patients to leave one, and reply to the ones that come in, good and bad. Keep your replies polite and general, and don't discuss anyone's treatment in public.

Keep the AHPRA line in mind too. Reviews on your Google listing are one thing. Taking those reviews and using them as testimonials about clinical care in your own advertising is something AHPRA doesn't allow. Collect reviews, don't repurpose them.

## Then check what the assistants actually say

Once the five fixes are in, ask the assistants the questions your patients would ask. Who's a good dentist in your suburb? Who does implants nearby? Who can see someone today? Note whether you're named, and whether they describe you correctly.

When they get something wrong, there's almost always a source behind it, an old listing or a vague page, and that's what you fix. Then check again later, because the answers change.
