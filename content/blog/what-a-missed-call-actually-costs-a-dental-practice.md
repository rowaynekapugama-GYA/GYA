A missed call costs a dental practice far more than one appointment, because most of the time it's a new patient who hangs up and rings the next practice on the list. You don't just lose the check-up they were calling about. You lose the treatment that might have come after it, the years of visits, and the family members and friends they'd have sent your way. The real cost is what one new patient is worth to you over time, multiplied by how many of those calls you miss. Most practices have never worked that number out, and it's usually bigger than they'd guess.

I'll be upfront about something. I'm not going to give you an industry figure for this, because the only number that matters is yours. A practice that does a lot of implants and one that's mostly check-ups and cleans will get very different answers. So instead, here's how to work it out properly in about half an hour.

## Why a missed call is worse than a missed email

When someone emails or fills in a form, there's a chance they'll wait for a reply. When someone rings, they want to talk to a person right now. If nobody picks up, very few leave a voicemail. They go back to Google and tap the next number.

Think about when people ring a dentist. Lunch breaks. On the way to work. Straight after a tooth starts hurting at 6pm. Those are exactly the times your front desk is busy with patients at the counter, on another line, or has gone home. It's not anyone's fault. It's just when the phone rings.

## How to work out your own number

You need four things. Most of them are already in your practice software or your phone system.

1. **How many calls you miss.** Your phone provider can usually show you missed and unanswered calls for the last month. Look at the times too. You'll probably see clusters at lunch and after hours.
2. **How many of those were new patients.** Check the missed numbers against your patient list. Any number that isn't in there is likely someone new. It won't be perfect, but it's close enough.
3. **How many new callers usually book.** Of the new patients who do get through to your team, roughly how many end up with an appointment? Your front desk will have a feel for this, and your booking records will confirm it.
4. **What a new patient is worth to you.** Not the first visit. The average spend of a new patient over their first year or two, including the treatment that follows. Your practice software can pull this.

Then multiply it out: missed new patient calls, times the share that would normally book, times what a new patient is worth. That's roughly what the missed calls cost you in a month. Times it by twelve and have a sit down.

## The cost you don't see on the report

There's a second cost that won't show up in that sum. If you're paying for marketing, some of those missed calls came from it.

Say someone sees your [Google Ads](../dental-google-ads.html), taps the call button and gets no answer. You paid for that click. The ad did its job. The patient was ready. Then the call went nowhere. When practice owners tell me their ads "don't work", one of the first things I check is how many of the calls the ads produced actually got answered.

This is why we measure success in booked patients, not clicks or calls. A call that nobody picks up isn't a result, however good it looks in the ad account.

## What you can do about it

Some of this is simple and costs nothing.

- **Check your call handling at your busy times.** Ring your own practice at 12:30pm and again at 5:45pm. See what a patient hears.
- **Make the voicemail useful.** Tell people when they'll get a call back and give them another way to reach you, like an online booking link.
- **Call missed numbers back the same day.** Have someone go through the missed call list at set times, not whenever there's a spare moment.
- **Stagger lunch breaks** so the phone is never left with nobody near it.

These help, but they all depend on a person being free at the right moment, and in a busy practice that won't always happen.
