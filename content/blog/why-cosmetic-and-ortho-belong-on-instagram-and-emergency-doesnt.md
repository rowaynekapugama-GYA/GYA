Instagram ads work for cosmetic and orthodontic treatment because those are decisions people sit on for weeks or months, and Instagram is where they spend that time looking. Emergency dental care is the opposite. Someone with a broken tooth or a swollen face isn't waiting for an ad to scroll past. They're on Google, typing "emergency dentist near me" and calling whoever answers first. So the simple rule is: put the treatments people think about on Instagram and Facebook, and put the treatments people need right now on Google. Mix them up and you pay for attention from people who were never going to book.

I see this mix-up a lot when a practice shows us their ad accounts. There's an Instagram campaign pushing emergency appointments, and a Google campaign trying to sell whitening to people who searched for a toothache. Neither is a disaster on its own. Together they're a slow leak.

## Two kinds of patient, two kinds of search

It helps to think about where someone's head is at when they see your ad.

**The planner.** They've been thinking about their smile for a while. Maybe it's a wedding next year, maybe it's teeth that have bugged them since high school. They're not in a rush. They look at photos, save posts, ask friends, and come back to it a few times before they do anything. This is cosmetic work, clear aligners and orthodontic treatment, and often implants too.

**The emergency.** Something hurts or broke, and they want it sorted today. They don't browse. They search, they look at the map, and they ring the first practice that looks open and close.

Instagram and Facebook are built for the planner. People are there to look at things, and an ad that shows a real clinic, a real team and a clear next step fits right into that. Google is built for the emergency. The person has already said what they want, in their own words, so you just need to be the answer.

## Why emergency ads on Instagram waste money

An emergency ad on Instagram is talking to thousands of people who don't have a dental emergency. The tiny number who do won't remember your ad when it happens weeks later. They'll search.

You end up paying to show "Same day emergency appointments" to people who are fine. It gets likes, maybe a few clicks, and very few bookings. It also tends to sit next to the cosmetic ads, which muddies what your practice looks like it's about.

Keep emergency where the intent is. On Google, an ad for "emergency dentist" plus your suburb reaches someone at the exact moment they need you. ## Why cosmetic and ortho do well on Instagram

Planners need to see things more than once. Instagram lets you stay in front of them while they make up their mind, without paying a search price every time.

A few things that work:

1. **Shoot it in your own clinic.** Your rooms, your team, your front desk. People want to know what it'll feel like to walk in. Stock photos of a perfect smile tell them nothing about you.
2. **Show the process, not just the result.** What happens at the first appointment, how long a consult takes, what the next step is. That's the stuff people actually wonder about.
3. **Target locally.** Most people won't travel far for a dentist, even for cosmetic work. Keep the audience to the suburbs you really draw from.
4. **Retarget people who showed interest.** Someone who watched your aligner video or visited the treatment page is worth showing a follow-up to.

## The AHPRA part

This matters more on Instagram than anywhere else, because social ads lean on pictures and stories.

Before and after photos, patient stories and glowing comments about treatment can all cross the line. You can't use testimonials about clinical care in your advertising, and you can't make claims that create unreasonable expectations. Offers need clear terms. And only registered specialists can call themselves orthodontists or specialists in ads, so a practice offering orthodontic treatment or clear aligners should describe the treatment, not borrow the title. Plenty of practices offer orthodontic treatment, and describing it accurately is all you need.

Everything we run is written to AHPRA advertising guidelines before it goes live. It's slower than just boosting a post, and it's the right way to do it.

## How to split your budget

You don't need an exact formula to start. Ask two questions about each treatment you want to grow:

- **Is it urgent?** If people need it today, it goes on Google.
- **Do people think about it for a while?** If yes, it suits Instagram and Facebook, with Google catching the people who are ready to search.
