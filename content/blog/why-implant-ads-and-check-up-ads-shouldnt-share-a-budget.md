Implant ads and check-up ads shouldn't share a budget because they're chasing completely different patients, and Google doesn't know which one you care about more. Put them in one campaign and Google spends the money wherever clicks are easiest to get, which is usually the high volume, lower value searches like "dentist near me" or "check-up and clean". The implant searches, the ones you set the account up for, get whatever is left. Give each treatment its own campaign and its own budget, and you decide where the money goes instead of the algorithm.

Here's what actually happens when they're lumped together, and how we set it up instead.

## What goes wrong when everything shares one pot

A lot of the dental accounts I look at have one campaign called something like "Dental" or "Search". Inside it are ads for implants, Invisalign, emergency, whitening and general check-ups, all drawing from the same daily budget.

It looks tidy. The problem is how Google spends that budget. It's trying to get you the most results for the money, and it doesn't know that one implant patient is worth far more to your practice than one check-up. So it leans toward the searches that are cheap and plentiful.

Check-up searches happen all day, every day. Implant searches are fewer, more considered, and usually cost more per click. Left to share a pot, the check-ups win most mornings. By the time someone in your area searches for implants that afternoon, the budget might already be gone.

You end up paying for the patients you'd probably have got anyway, and missing the ones you actually set the ads up to find.

## Different treatments, different patients

It's not just about money. Someone looking for a check-up and someone looking into implants are in very different headspaces.

The check-up patient wants someone nearby, open at a time that suits them, who takes their health fund. They'll often book on the first call.

The implant patient is doing research. They want to understand what's involved, how long it takes and how to get started. They might read a few pages, look at reviews, and come back a week later.

One ad and one landing page can't speak to both of those people properly. When they each have their own campaign, they each get ads written for them and a page that answers their questions.

## How we split it

Here's the structure we use on every dental account we build.

1. **One campaign per treatment.** Implants, Invisalign, emergency, cosmetic and general each get their own campaign. You can add or drop treatments based on what you want more of.
2. **One budget per campaign.** Each treatment has its own daily spend, so a busy week of check-up searches can't touch the money set aside for implants.
3. **Built around your suburbs.** We target the suburbs your patients actually come from, per treatment. Some patients will travel further for implants than they would for a clean, so the areas don't have to match.
4. **Negative keywords from day one.** Each campaign gets its own list, so implant ads don't show for "free dental check-up" and check-up ads don't show for "implant cost overseas". Then we keep adding to those lists every week.
5. **Each click lands on the right page.** Implant ads go to your implant page. Check-up ads go to your general page. Never the homepage.

If you want the bigger picture of how we run accounts, it's all on our [Google Ads for dentists](../dental-google-ads.html) page.

## Is splitting it more expensive?

No. It's the same money, just pointed where you want it. Most clinics we manage put between 3% and 8% of revenue into marketing, mostly Google Ads and SEO. Splitting campaigns doesn't change that number. It changes what you get for it.

It does take more work to run: more ads, more search terms to check, more negative keywords. A single campaign is easier for whoever manages it, not better for you.

## What to look at in your own account

You can check this yourself in about ten minutes.

- Open Google Ads and look at how many campaigns you have. If it's one or two, everything is probably sharing.
- Look at the search terms report. See how much of your spend went on general searches compared to the treatment you most want to grow.
- Check where your ads send people. If it's the homepage, that's the next thing to fix.

If most of the money is going to searches you didn't really set the account up for, you've found your leak.

## Keep the ads on the right side of AHPRA

One thing to watch when you're writing treatment ads: AHPRA's advertising rules apply to every one. No testimonials about clinical care, nothing that sets up unreasonable expectations, and no offers without clear terms. That matters more on treatments like implants, where it's tempting to promise too much in a headline. We write every ad and landing page to those rules before it goes live.
