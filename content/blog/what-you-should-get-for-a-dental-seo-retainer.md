For a dental SEO retainer you should get ongoing work on your Google map listing, a proper page for each treatment you want more of, a steady flow of reviews, the technical fixes that keep your site fast and readable, and reporting that tells you how many enquiries and bookings came in. You should also get a person you can talk to regularly, and a clear idea of what's being done each month. If all you get is a monthly PDF full of rankings and impressions, you're paying for a report, not for SEO.

I get asked some version of this a lot. A practice owner has been paying someone for SEO for a year or two, and when I ask what's actually been done, they're not sure. They get an email each month. The charts go up and to the right. But the phones don't feel any busier.

That's usually not because SEO doesn't work for dentists. It's because nobody set out what the money was meant to buy.

## The work that should be in it

Here's what I'd expect from any dental SEO retainer, ours or anyone else's.

### Your map listing, kept alive

Your Google Business Profile is where most patients first see you when they search "dentist near me". It should have the right primary category, every treatment listed as a service in the words patients use, real photos of your team and rooms, and hours that are right, including public holidays.

That's the setup. The retainer part is keeping it alive: new photos, updated services, posts when something changes, and checking nothing has been edited by someone else. A listing filled in once and left alone slowly drifts down.

### A page for every treatment you want to grow

If you want more implant patients, you need a page that's properly about implants. Same for Invisalign, emergency appointments, check-ups, whatever you want more of. Each one should answer what patients ask: what's involved, roughly how long it takes, and how to book.

Over a year, you should be able to point at the pages that were written or rebuilt. If you can't, ask why.

### Your details matching everywhere

Your practice name, address and phone number should be identical on your website, your listing, health fund directories and anywhere else you're listed. It's dull work and it's one of the most common reasons a good practice sits lower than it should. A retainer should include finding and fixing these.

### Reviews coming in steadily

A few reviews every month beats a big push once a year. Your agency should help set up a simple way for patients to leave one, and help you reply. And they should know AHPRA's line: patients can review you, but those reviews can't be reused as testimonials about clinical care in your ads. If an agency suggests otherwise, that's a red flag.

### The technical work underneath

Fast on a phone, a clean site structure, and the right schema so Google and AI assistants can tell exactly what you do and where. Ask what's been checked and fixed.

### Writing for AI search too

Patients now ask ChatGPT and Google's AI questions like "do I need a crown or a filling?" or "who can see me today?". Pages that answer clearly in the first few lines are the ones those assistants quote. That's part of SEO now, not an extra.

## The reporting you should get

This is where most retainers fall down. Rankings and impressions are easy to report and they always look like progress. They don't tell you whether the books are fuller.

You should be able to see how many enquiries came from search, which treatments they were for, and how many of them booked. That's the number that tells you whether the retainer is paying for itself. We measure success in booked patients, not impressions or clicks, and I think any practice owner should ask for the same.

You should also get a real conversation, not just a document. We have a call with every client every fortnight to go through the numbers together.

## What a fair timeline looks like

Be wary of anyone promising page one by Friday. In our experience, movement on the map usually shows in six to ten weeks. Competitive treatment terms like "dental implants" take longer, often four to nine months.

That gap is why a lot of our clients run [Google Ads](../dental-google-ads.html) at the start. Ads can bring enquiries in within days while the SEO builds. If your retainer has been running for six months and nothing has moved on the map, that's worth a direct conversation.

## Questions to ask before you sign

1. What will you do in the first month, and what will you do every month after that?
2. Which treatment pages will you write or rebuild, and when?
3. How will you report enquiries and bookings, not just rankings?
4. How often will I talk to a real person about my account?
5. Do you work with other dental practices near me?

That last one matters more than people think. When you're trying to rank for "dentist" plus your suburb, you're competing with the practices closest to you for the same three spots on the map. We won't take on two dental clinics within 7km of each other, so if we're working on your listing and treatment pages, we're not doing the same job for the practice up the road. Ask any agency where they draw that line, and get the answer in writing.
