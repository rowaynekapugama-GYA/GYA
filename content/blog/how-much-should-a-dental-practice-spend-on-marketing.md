Most of the dental clinics we manage put between 3% and 8% of their revenue into marketing, and most of that goes on Google Ads and SEO. Where you should sit in that range depends on what you're trying to do. A settled practice that mostly wants to keep the books full can sit near the bottom. A practice trying to grow a particular treatment, open a second chair or get noticed in a busy area will usually need to sit higher, at least for a while. The percentage is a starting point, though. The number that actually matters is what it costs you to get a new patient booked in, and whether that's worth it.

I get asked this on almost every first call. Usually it's phrased as "what do other practices spend?", which is a fair question. You want to know if you're way off. So here's the honest version.

## Why it's a range and not one number

Two practices with the same revenue can need very different budgets.

One has been in the same spot for twenty years, gets plenty of referrals from existing patients, and just wants a steady trickle of new ones. The other opened recently, or wants to do more implants or Invisalign, or sits in a suburb where five other practices are fighting over the same searches. The second one has more ground to make up, so it needs to spend more to get there.

That's why we don't hand out a single figure. We look at where you are, what you want more of, and how crowded your area is, then work out where in that 3% to 8% range makes sense.

## Where the money usually goes

For most of our clients it splits between Google Ads and SEO, and the split changes over time. Here's why.

Google Ads can bring enquiries in within days. SEO takes longer. Movement on the Google map usually shows in six to ten weeks, and competitive treatment terms like "dental implants" often take four to nine months to rank for.

So early on, more of the budget tends to sit in ads, because that's what fills the book while the [SEO work](../dental-seo.html) builds. As your treatment pages and map listing start pulling their weight, you can often ease off the ads for the searches you now rank for, and put that money towards the treatments that still need help.

Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram) come into it too, mostly for treatments people think about for a while before booking. But for most practices, Google is where the bulk of the budget does its work, because that's where people go when they're ready to book.

## Work it out from patients, not percentages

The percentage tells you if you're in a sensible zone. It doesn't tell you if the money is working. For that you need three numbers:

1. **What a new patient is worth to you.** Not one visit. What they typically spend with you over the time they stay, including treatment that follows the first appointment.
2. **How many new patients you want each month.** Be specific, and split it by treatment if you can. Ten more check-ups and two more implant cases are very different goals.
3. **What it costs you now to get one booked in.** Total marketing spend for the month divided by new patients who actually booked.

If the cost to book a patient is well under what they're worth, you can probably spend more and grow faster. If it's close to or over, spending more just loses money faster, and something needs fixing first.

That's why we measure everything in booked patients, not clicks or impressions. A cheap click that never turns into a booking isn't cheap.

## The leaks that make any budget look too small

Before you raise the budget, check where it's leaking. These are the ones we see most.

**Enquiries that don't get a reply.** Someone fills in a form at 9pm or calls while the front desk is with a patient, and nobody gets back to them until the next day. By then they've often booked somewhere else. This is exactly why we built [SmileOX](../smileox.html), our own lead platform. It replies to new enquiries from any source straight away, aiming for a first response in under 60 seconds, texts back missed calls, and passes people to your team when they need a human.

**Ads that land on the homepage.** Someone searches for implants and lands on a page about everything. They have to go looking, and plenty won't. We build [Google Ads](../dental-google-ads.html) per treatment and per suburb, landing on the matching treatment page.

**Paying for the wrong searches.** Without a negative keyword list, an implant ad can show for "dental implant courses" or "dental assistant jobs". We set those lists up from day one.

Fixing these often does more than adding budget. The same spend books more patients.

## Where to start

Work out those three numbers for last month: what a new patient is worth, how many you got, and what it cost to get each one. Then compare your total spend to your revenue.

If you're under 3% and quiet, there's probably room to grow. If you're over 8% and it doesn't feel like it's working, the problem is usually a leak, not the budget.
