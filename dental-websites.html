Why this matters

Your website is the most expensive thing in your practice that nobody measures.

You know what a chair costs and what your lab bill was last month. Almost no owner can tell you what their website costs them, because it never arrives as an invoice. It arrives as patients who never called.

A three second load and they've tapped the next practice on the list. A booking button three taps deep and the patient who'd already decided is gone. Implants listed in eleven words on a services page, so when someone searches for implants in your suburb, Google has nothing of yours to show. And a site that looks like 2016, being read by someone about to spend fifteen thousand dollars on their mouth.

None of it shows up on a report. All of it shows up in your diary.

In every build

The boring stuff, done properly.

The prettiest dental site in Australia is worthless if it scores 40 on mobile and nobody can work out how to book.

  • Speed first

    Your site should be up and usable in under two and a half seconds, with nothing shifting around underneath a thumb that's already going to tap. Photos and video get shrunk down so they don't hold the page up. This is what decides whether the patient standing in a car park with two bars waits for you or taps the next result.

  • Mobile first

    We design the phone version first and scale it up. Every decision has to earn its place in a space the size of a hand, and click-to-call gets tested on a real device.

  • Video banners

    Clinic walk-throughs and team footage, shot at your practice. Dental fear is about the room, and a video answers it in eight seconds. We shrink the file right down and load it a moment after the rest of the page, so it looks cinematic without making anyone wait.

  • Booking and SmileOX

    Booking on every page, not buried on a contact page. Then every enquiry lands in SmileOX and gets answered in under a minute, day or night, instead of sitting in an inbox until Monday.

  • Built to rank

    A treatment in a list ranks for nothing. A page about it can rank for years and cost nothing per click. So implants, Invisalign and emergency each get one, plus the suburbs you actually draw from, with schema built in during the build.

  • Accessible and compliant

    Proper contrast and keyboard navigation, and every word written to the AHPRA advertising rules. Seven years of dental copy means you're not proofreading your own site for compliance.

  • Copy written for you

    Treatment pages, suburb pages and FAQs, in your voice. This is what stalls most website projects for six weeks. We write them, you approve them, it ships.

A fair question

Can't we just use a template?

For some businesses, yes. For a practice competing on local search, rarely. A template gives you a services page instead of treatment pages, code you don't need slowing the whole thing down, no schema and no suburb strategy, so you rank for your practice name and nothing else. None of that is visible on day one. All of it is visible when you start paying for ads to make up for a site that can't earn its own traffic.

It also can't give you someone who knows dental, and knows which words get you a letter from AHPRA.
